LLM Council Report

Proxy · myproxy.work · May 2026

Should Proxy pursue AEO (Agent Engine Optimisation) positioning now — and how aggressively?

⚖️ Chairman's Verdict

Where the Council Agrees

All five advisors agreed on three things without exception: (1) add schema.org JSON-LD Person markup to portfolio pages this week — 2-3 hours, already have the pattern from the blog system, zero downside. (2) Build the post-payment share modal — one-day build, pre-written LinkedIn copy, one button. (3) AEO as a long-term thesis is directionally correct. Not wrong — just early.

Where the Council Clashes

The Expansionist says name the category now, go loud, pursue recruiter B2B. The Contrarian says you have four paying users — category creation is a VC story, not a growth lever. Both are right about different time horizons. This is a sequencing problem, not a strategic disagreement.

Does AEO go on the landing page now? The Expansionist says yes, loudly. The Outsider says "Built for humans. Found by AI." is already the strongest line — use it as the hook. The Contrarian says don't rebuild positioning around a concept users don't know yet. The verdict: the tagline is usable now. The concept AEO does not go on the landing page as a heading. It goes in the copy as supporting proof, one level deeper.

Blind Spots the Council Caught

All five peer reviews flagged the same miss: nobody examined the moment the product asks for money. Nine people cared enough to complete a profile. The gap is between "your profile is ready" and "$49." That is the highest-leverage diagnostic in this entire conversation and none of the primary advisors ran it.

One peer review raised a structural issue none of the advisors questioned: the one-time pricing model. At $49 one-time, 25 paying users = $1,225 in total revenue. That is not a business — it's a proof of concept. A recurring model at $9–15/month changes the unit economics entirely and makes "25 paying users" mean something durable rather than a number that resets to zero each month.

The Recommendation

Do the schema markup and share modal — already decided, correct, fast. Do not rebuild the landing page around AEO as a concept. Use "Built for humans. Found by AI." as the tagline on the existing page. That is enough positioning work for now.

Before emailing the nine free users, spend one hour understanding why they did not pay. Look at where they dropped — did they see the payment prompt? Did they complete the questionnaire? Is there a clear upgrade moment? If you can get two of them on a ten-minute call, do it. That conversation is worth more than any of the three product moves.

Then reconsider the pricing model. One-time $49 with no recurring revenue means every month starts from zero. Even a light recurring option — $9/month or an annual plan — changes the trajectory.

The AEO recruiter B2B angle is the most interesting long-term move in this entire council. File it. Return to it once you understand why free users aren't converting.

🎯 The One Thing to Do First

Talk to two free users before writing a single line of code or copy. Ask one question: "You built your profile — what stopped you from paying $49?" That answer makes every other decision in this council either irrelevant or obvious.

Advisor Responses
The Contrarian — AEO is infrastructure for a world that doesn't reliably exist yet
The fatal flaw: AEO is not a proven recruitment channel yet. You're building infrastructure for a world that doesn't reliably exist. AI agents sourcing candidates at scale is a thesis, not a fact. Recruiters still use LinkedIn, ATS systems, and referrals. Schema.org Person markup on a small, low-domain-authority site won't surface a candidate above their LinkedIn profile in any AI-powered search tool today.

The positioning problem is worse. "Built for humans. Found by AI" is abstract to a mid-senior professional who needs a job now. They don't know what AEO means. They don't care about structured data. They care about getting interviews.

The distraction risk is real. You have 4 paying users. The gap between 4 and 25 is not a positioning gap — it's a distribution and trust gap. What will actually get to 25 paying users: direct outreach to 50 mid-senior professionals, one sharp testimonial from a user who got a recruiter response, and a referral mechanism.

Do the schema markup — it takes two hours and has zero downside. Skip rebuilding the positioning around AEO. Don't distract from the real problem: nobody knows Proxy exists yet.
The First Principles Thinker — You're asking the wrong question. Ask why 9 free users haven't paid.
The surface question is "should we add AEO features?" The real question is: what is the actual blocker to going from 4 to 25 paying users?

You have 13 users. 4 paid. That is a 31% conversion rate on a tiny sample. Before asking "how do we get found by AI agents," ask "why aren't the 9 free users paying?" You don't have a discoverability problem yet. You have a conversion and acquisition problem. AEO solves neither of those right now.

The schema markup is not a distraction — it's a 2-hour implementation that costs almost nothing and the thesis has a real foundation. AI sourcing is directionally correct as a bet. But the positioning is a 2027 value proposition dressed up as a 2025 differentiator.

The distribution nudge is the highest-leverage move of the three. Build that regardless of AEO framing. Do not invest sessions building AEO as a core product story until you understand why 9 people took the time to build a profile and did not pay $49. That answer is worth more than any positioning framework right now.
The Expansionist — This is a category-creation moment. Pursue it loudly.
This is a category-creation moment, not a feature add. First-mover advantage in naming a category is worth more than any feature roadmap. The real upside isn't helping 25 people get found by AI agents. It's becoming the canonical format for AI-readable professional identity. If Proxy defines what a machine-readable career profile looks like, every other tool has to reference you.

The schema markup is a one-session build. Do it this week. But go further. The distribution nudge should say "Share your AI-ready profile" — not just "share your profile." Every user who posts to LinkedIn with that framing is a walking press release for the category.

The adjacent opportunity nobody's talking about: recruiters. Recruiters using AI sourcing tools right now don't have a structured feed of mid-to-senior candidates. Proxy could be the supply side of that equation. One recruiter partnership — even informal — validates the entire AEO thesis and opens a B2B revenue channel that dwarfs $49 one-time payments.

Pursue it loudly. The window to own "AEO for professionals" is open right now. It won't be in 18 months.
The Outsider — AEO is jargon to your buyer. The tagline is already the answer.
The positioning is almost invisible to a fresh reader. "AI-readable professional identity" and "Agent Engine Optimisation" are insider phrases. Someone landing on the site for the first time — a mid-level marketing director, a finance manager, a procurement lead — will not know what AEO means. They are thinking: "I hate my resume. I hate job hunting. I want someone to find me."

The tagline "Built for humans. Found by AI." is the clearest thing in this entire pitch. Lead with that. Everything else is explanatory scaffolding that belongs one click deeper.

Schema markup — do it quietly this week. Invisible to users, low effort. The distribution nudge is the only move that directly affects reaching 25 paying users — the other two require Google, Perplexity, and ChatGPT to change how they surface candidates. That is a long bet. The nudge is a short bet.

The risk is not that AEO is a bad idea. The risk is that it becomes a story you tell yourself instead of solving the real problem: thirteen users, most of them free, and no clear reason why someone pays $49 today versus tomorrow. Fix the conversion problem first.
The Executor — Email the 9 free users today. The rest is sequenced after that.
Move 1 is a Monday morning task. Schema.org Person markup on portfolio pages — 2-3 hours. Drop it into the head as JSON-LD. Already have the pattern from the blog system. Do it this week.

Move 3 is a one-day build. After payment confirms, show a modal: "Your profile is live. Share it." Pre-write the LinkedIn copy. One button. Done.

Move 2 is already in progress — don't count it as a new task.

The AEO positioning is marketing copy, not a product move. Write it on the landing page after Move 1. Costs nothing.

Getting from 9 free to 25 paid is a distribution problem, not a product problem. The fastest path: email the 9 free users this week with an upgrade hook. That's a one-hour task. The structured data gives you a concrete, credible thing to say: "Your profile is now machine-readable by AI agents." That's a real upgrade hook.

Priority order: 1. Email 9 free users with upgrade hook. 2. Add JSON-LD Person markup. 3. Add share modal post-payment. 4. Update landing page copy. Don't build anything else until you've hit 25.
